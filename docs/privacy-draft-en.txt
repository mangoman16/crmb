2. Purposes
We use information in the badminton portal to organise training, manage memberships and payments, and communicate with students and their contact people. Each student has an account of their own, and each account belongs to exactly one student; it can be used to sign in once it has an email address of its own. Whoever uses it can view that student’s information and edit the fields made available to them.

3. Information processed
The portal processes account details, student names, optional dates of birth, contact people and their phone numbers or email addresses, membership and tariff information, charges, payments, optional absence reports and messages. Passwords are stored as hashes. SMTP passwords and queued email contents are encrypted in the application database.
People sign in with their own email address. The portal sends no email to an account without an email address, so no invoices, reminders or password-reset links either.

Anyone who reports a problem through “Something is wrong on this page” sends a description of their own and, if they wish, a screenshot. With it the portal stores the account the report comes from, the device, browser and language setting as the browser reports them, the IP address, and the last pages opened in the portal, up to eight, with what was entered in forms sent from them; of uploaded files, only their size and type. Passwords and security codes are never stored. Until a report is sent, this list is kept only in the session on the server, and it is deleted on signing out. A report is used only to reproduce and fix the fault, and only the portal’s administrators can see it. Once it is marked as done, the portal deletes what was entered in the forms that were sent; the description, screenshot, device details, IP address and the list of pages opened, with their addresses and any search term, are kept for 30 more days; then the report is deleted entirely, together with its screenshot. Open reports are kept until they are dealt with.
When an unexpected error happens in the portal, the portal records it itself, without anyone having to report it: which error it was and where in the program, the address of the page, the signed-in account and its role, the device and browser as the browser reports them, and the last pages opened with that account before it, up to eight, with what was entered in forms sent from them. The IP address is not stored with it, and passwords and security codes never are. Only the portal’s administrators can see such an entry, and it is used only to find and fix the fault. If outside help is needed for that, a summary can be passed on that contains no names, email addresses or IP addresses and nothing that was entered. Once the entry is marked as done, the portal deletes what was entered in the forms; at the latest 30 days after the error last happened, the entry is deleted entirely.
Messages: every course has a group chat. Its messages are read by everyone currently enrolled in the course and by the trainers and administrators; somebody who joins the course can also read the group’s earlier messages, and somebody who leaves it no longer can. In the group, members see each other’s names and initials and, where the family has agreed, the profile picture. Trainers and administrators can hide a message in a group; its content stays stored for 30 days so that it can be restored, and is then deleted. The club’s administrators can read every direct chat – one between a student account and a trainer as much as one between two trainers – but write only in their own; an open direct chat says so at the top. The portal does not record whether or when an administrator read along. There are no new direct chats between two student accounts; earlier ones stay readable for the two of them and the administrators, and take no new messages. Children in a course whose family has no access to the portal yet are not shown to the other children by name. A message holds text, a photo or both; a photo is the only thing a student account uploads in the chat. Voice notes and files sent earlier stay stored and readable like the other messages. From photos that are uploaded, the portal removes where and when they were taken and the camera details before storing them; only which way up the picture is stays. A photo it cannot read through for this, and a GIF, such as one sent as a payment proof, are stored as they came. For a photo in a chat, the portal does not store the file name the device gave it either.

Profile pictures: a child can have a profile picture. The family, through the child’s account on the child’s page, and the trainers and administrators, also at training, can add, replace and delete it; it is never required. Of it, the portal keeps only a small square picture of 320 × 320 pixels, without where or when it was taken and without the camera details; the photo itself is not kept. A child’s profile picture is seen by the club’s trainers and administrators and by the child’s own account. The legal basis for this is the club’s legitimate interest in recognising who is in the course, for attendance and the children’s safety (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). The children in the child’s courses and their families see it in the course chat only if the family has switched that on on the child’s page; the legal basis is then consent (Art. 6 (1) (a) GDPR). From the age of 14 the child decides this alone; below that, and without a date of birth on record, a parent does (§ 4 (4) DSG). A parent’s consent stays valid when the child turns 14, and the child can then withdraw it. It can be switched off at any time, with immediate effect. A picture added by a trainer or an administrator is shown to the course only after a new consent. A family that does not consent still sees the others’ pictures. Trainers and administrators can add a profile picture of their own, voluntarily, which everybody signed in to the portal sees; the legal basis is their consent (Art. 6 (1) (a) GDPR), which they withdraw by deleting the picture. A picture that is deleted or replaced is deleted by the portal at once, as is a child’s picture when the child is deleted, and a team member’s with their account. A browser that has already shown a picture keeps a copy of it for up to a week, but no page shows it any more; on signing out, the portal asks the browser to delete such copies. No profile picture appears in emails, notifications, invoices or pages for people who are not signed in.

Email notices about new messages, changed training dates and answers to requests have a separate preference. Security emails such as invitations, password links and the notice that the sign-in address or the password was changed provide and protect account access.

5. Recipients and services
The training management team and the student’s own account can access information according to their permissions. Other student accounts cannot access it, except for the group chat of a course they share, as described under Messages.
The information is never sold, never rented, and never passed to anybody else for advertising. It is used only inside the club, for running the training: courses, memberships, charges and the messages about them. It is passed on only where the portal technically needs it to work (hosting, sending email) or where the law requires it.

6. Retention and deletion
The portal deletes on its own, in its daily cleanup, whatever is older than its period: messages with their photos, voice notes and files 12 months after they were sent, and a hidden message 30 days after it was hidden; absences, sick notes included, 3 months after they ended; attendance entries 24 months after the training day; notifications in the bell after 90 days; the record of the portal’s emails – to whom, about what, when – after 12 months, the text of a sent email after 90 days already, that of a security email as soon as it is sent, and that of an email that could not be sent with its entry in the record; uploaded payment proofs 24 months after the upload; the log of who did what in the portal and when, without any content, after 36 months; an answer to a consent question 36 months after a newer one replaced it, while the answer in force stays as long as the account exists; the log of changes to stored details after 24 months; problem reports 30 days after they were dealt with, and errors recorded automatically 30 days after they last occurred.
Charges, payments and invoices are kept for seven years from the end of the calendar year they concern, and longer while a pending proceeding needs them (§ 132 (1) BAO); the portal never deletes them on its own. Deleted entries remain in the last five backup copies of the database until newer copies replace them; a new copy is made before an update changes the database. Photos and other uploaded files are not in these copies.

Deleting an account removes the login and its private conversations. The student it belonged to and their payment records remain for separate handling. Suspending an account prevents access without deleting student information.

7. Necessary technical information
The portal uses a necessary session cookie for sign-in, language and form protection. When it sends a browser on to its encrypted address, it also sets the cookie “badminton_https”, equally necessary: it records for a year that this browser is connected encrypted, and holds no personal information. It sets no other cookies. It does not load external fonts, analytics or advertising services. Temporary technical records limit login attempts and prevent duplicate form submissions.
The server of our hosting provider writes a line to an access log for every request to the portal: the IP address, the date and time, the address requested, whether the request succeeded, the amount of data transferred, the browser and operating system as the browser reports them and, when the portal is opened from another website, that website’s address if the browser sends it. The portal’s own pages tell the browser not to pass on their address. Faults in the portal are also written to the server’s error log. These logs are deleted after 30 days. We look at them only when a fault has to be found and fixed or an attack has to be investigated; they are not analysed for statistics, usage profiles, advertising or any other purpose. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: our legitimate interest is running the portal securely and without faults, and we have no further interest in this data.
