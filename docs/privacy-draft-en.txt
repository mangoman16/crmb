2. Purposes
We use information in the badminton portal to organise training, manage memberships and payments, and communicate with students and their contact people. Each student has an account of their own, and each account belongs to exactly one student; it can be used to sign in once it has an email address or a username. Whoever uses it can view that student’s information and edit the fields made available to them.

3. Information processed
The portal processes account details, student names, optional dates of birth, contact people and their phone numbers or email addresses, membership and tariff information, charges, payments, optional absence reports, messages and additional information explicitly requested in custom fields. Passwords are stored as hashes. SMTP passwords and queued email contents are encrypted in the application database.
People sign in with their email address or, for a student account without an email address of its own, with a username. The portal sends no email to an account without an email address, so no invoices, reminders or password-reset links either. Trainers and administrators can create a sign-in link for a student account: it works once and for at most 48 hours; whoever uses it sets a new password with it. It is shown as a QR code or passed on by them, for example through a messenger, but never sent by email. The portal keeps only a one-way fingerprint of the link, who created it and when, and when it was used or withdrawn.

Anyone who reports a problem through “Something is wrong on this page” sends a description of their own and, if they wish, a screenshot. With it the portal stores the account the report comes from, the device, browser and language setting as the browser reports them, the IP address, and the last pages opened in the portal, up to eight, with what was entered in forms sent from them; of uploaded files, only their size and type. Passwords and security codes are never stored. Until a report is sent, this list is kept only in the session on the server, and it is deleted on signing out. A report is used only to reproduce and fix the fault, and only the portal’s administrators can see it. Once it is marked as done, the portal deletes what was entered in the forms that were sent; the description, screenshot, device details, IP address and the list of pages opened, with their addresses and any search term, are kept for 30 more days; then the report is deleted entirely, together with its screenshot. Open reports are kept until they are dealt with.
When an unexpected error happens in the portal, the portal records it itself, without anyone having to report it: which error it was and where in the program, the address of the page, the signed-in account and its role, the device and browser as the browser reports them, and the last pages opened with that account before it, up to eight, with what was entered in forms sent from them. The IP address is not stored with it, and passwords and security codes never are. Only the portal’s administrators can see such an entry, and it is used only to find and fix the fault. If outside help is needed for that, a summary can be passed on that contains no names, email addresses or IP addresses and nothing that was entered. Once the entry is marked as done, the portal deletes what was entered in the forms; at the latest 30 days after the error last happened, the entry is deleted entirely.
For every account the portal stores when it was last opened and, for at most the last 30 days, from when to when it was open – only the date and time, no IP address and no pages viewed. Only trainers and administrators can see this; student accounts see neither their own times nor anyone else’s. These periods are deleted automatically once they are older than 30 days, or sooner if the portal is set to a shorter period; the portal clears them out once a day. The time of the last visit is kept as long as the account exists and is deleted with it. A trainer who chooses “Appear offline” appears offline to other trainers; administrators still see the times. Whether somebody is online right now is shown by a coloured dot beside their name; every signed-in account sees this dot, student accounts included – but not when anybody was last here.
Messages: every course has a group chat. Its messages are read by everyone currently enrolled in the course and by the trainers and administrators; somebody who joins the course can also read the group’s earlier messages, and somebody who leaves it no longer can. In the group, members see each other’s names, initials or – for trainers and administrators – profile picture, a status emoji of their own choosing and the online dot. Trainers and administrators can hide a message in a group; its content stays stored so that it can be restored. Direct chats between a student account and a trainer or an administrator can be read by the administrators. Direct chats between two student accounts, after one has agreed to the other, are read only by the two of them. Children in a course whose family has no access to the portal yet are not shown to the other children by name. From photos that are uploaded, the portal removes where and when they were taken and the camera details before storing them; only which way up the picture is stays.

Email notifications for private messages have a separate preference. Security emails such as invitations and password links provide account access.

5. Recipients and services
The training management team and the student’s own account can access information according to their permissions. Other student accounts cannot access it, except for the group chat of a course they share, as described under Messages.
The information is never sold, never rented, and never passed to anybody else for advertising. It is used only inside the club, for running the training: courses, memberships, charges and the messages about them. It is passed on only where the portal technically needs it to work (hosting, sending email) or where the law requires it.

6. Retention and deletion
[Set specific retention periods or clear criteria for membership details, absences, messages, email logs, consent records, security logs and payment records. Account for applicable statutory retention duties.]
Deleting an account removes the login and its private conversations. The student it belonged to and their payment records remain for separate handling. Suspending an account prevents access without deleting student information.

7. Necessary technical information
The portal uses a necessary session cookie for sign-in, language and form protection, and sets no other cookies. It does not load external fonts, analytics or advertising services. Temporary technical records limit login attempts and prevent duplicate form submissions.
The server of our hosting provider writes a line to an access log for every request to the portal: the IP address, the date and time, the address requested, whether the request succeeded, the amount of data transferred, the browser and operating system as the browser reports them and, when the portal is opened from another website, that website’s address if the browser sends it. The portal’s own pages tell the browser not to pass on their address. Faults in the portal are also written to the server’s error log. These logs are deleted after 30 days. We look at them only when a fault has to be found and fixed or an attack has to be investigated; they are not analysed for statistics, usage profiles, advertising or any other purpose. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: our legitimate interest is running the portal securely and without faults, and we have no further interest in this data.
